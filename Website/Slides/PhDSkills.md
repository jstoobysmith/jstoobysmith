
## What skills are most important for a PhD student starting in 2026 to either protect or acquire?

Firstly I want to acknowledge that:
> Noone really knows the answer to this question.

The future is uncertain, and knowing what skills are going to be important are hard.

Though I understand this isn't the most helpful answer to this question.

I'm going to start with some general skills, and then go onto specific skills.

## 1. General skills

These are things I think everyone should work on.

*Knowledge of the field and the role of AI*
- Ground on a deep knowledge of the subject.
- Know the big questions in the area
- Know what determines a good answer to those questions
- Know how to ask useful questions, including follow up questions.
- Know what the capabilities are of AI in your area.
- Know how to distiquish between fact and hype.

*Use of AI*
- Learn how to use AI to make your lives easier.
- Learn how AI fits into your workflow.
- Learn how to get the most out of AI with differing availibities (don't become dependent on having access to the latest model).

*Community*
- Learn how to work will in collobrations
- Learn how to listen to others
- Learn how to give good talks
- Learn how to be a valued member of the community

## 2. Specific skills

- Anacdote: During a postdoc I did, I really struggled to find a academic position, so I started to look elsewhere. Naturally one of the skills I thought would be useful was to learn how to build AI systems. In summary, I hated it. I just didn't like that area, it didn't fill me with joy. So I looked for other skills instead (will get back to this).

The specific skills we learn natrually depend on your subject areas, and where our strengths lie. We work as a community, and it is important to work out how our own strengths can be best adapted to fit in within that community - no one needs to be good at everything.

Here are some new skills it might be worth a PhD student aquiring in a AI world:

- Building AI workflows.
- Fine tuning AI models.
- Building benchmarks.
- Evaulating how good a models and workflows are.
- Planning research projects, specification of requirements.
- Understanding of computer algebra systems, like Mathematica.
- Human in the loop solutions to mathematical and physics problems.
- Generationg and understanding of AI digests of the literature on a given area.
- Formalization.
- and many more.

## 3. More detail on formalization.


- After I decided I wasn't very good at this whole AI buisness, I decided to look into this thing called formalization. This was about 3 years ago, before it was big in mathematics, and it was basically non-existent in physics.

- Formalization means writing mathematical definitons and lemmas into lanagues like Lean: Ensures mathematical correctness and consistency is gaurenteed.
- Relevent to AI because you can have feedback loops between the AI and e.g. Lean.
- Sucess in mathematics driven by large open-source project Mathlib.
- Equivalent in Physics is Physlib. Community run project to formalize physics into Lean (not trying to axiomize).
- Promising future for physics.
- Need more people with this skill set to contribute to these sorts of projects.
